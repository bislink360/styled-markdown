Background

Our checkout was designed in 2019 for desktop web, when 70% of orders came from desktop. Today 64% of orders come from mobile, and the checkout has barely changed. Every quarterly survey since 2024 lists “checkout is slow / too many steps” among the top three complaints.

In the 2025 planning cycle we tried incremental fixes: address autocomplete (shipped March 2025, −1.2pp abandonment), a progress indicator (June 2025, no measurable change) and saved cards for logged-in users (September 2025, −0.8pp). Each helped a little, but the structural problem remained: four page loads, each with its own validation round trip, on mobile networks.

Competitor benchmarking in July 2026 (see the research deck) showed that 7 of our 10 closest competitors now use a single-page checkout, and 9 of 10 offer a wallet payment (Apple Pay or Google Pay) above the fold. The median competitor checkout completes in 38 seconds; ours takes 71.

Leadership agreed in the Q3 business review that checkout is the single largest conversion lever for FY27, and approved a dedicated squad for Q4.

👤For humans: Research highlights
  • 23 moderated usability sessions (12 mobile, 11 desktop) in August 2026.
  • Biggest drop-off: the shipping-method step (41% of abandons), mostly because shipping costs appear too late.
  • Users who saw the total price including shipping on the first screen were 1.6× more likely to complete.
  • “I didn’t know if my coupon worked” came up in 9 of 23 sessions.
  • Apple Pay users completed checkout in a median of 19 seconds in the prototype test.

Goals and non-goals

Goals

  1. Reduce checkout abandonment from 38% to under 30% within 8 weeks of GA.
  2. Median time-to-pay under 40 seconds on mobile.
  3. Wallet payments (Apple Pay, Google Pay) used in at least 25% of mobile orders.

Non-goals

  • Changing the cart page (separate initiative in Q1 2027).
  • New payment providers beyond our current PSP.
  • Guest-to-account conversion flows.

Design

The design explores three layouts (accordion, long scroll, two-column). Accordion won usability testing on mobile (completion 91% vs 84% and 86%) and is what we ship. Desktop uses a two-column variant: form on the left, sticky order summary on the right.

Design review notes (2026-09-04)
  • Sections expand one at a time; completed sections collapse to a one-line summary with an “Edit” link.
  • Error states: inline under the field, plus a summary at the top of the section on submit.
  • The order summary always shows subtotal, shipping (estimate or final), discount and total.
  • Wallet buttons follow Apple and Google brand guidelines; no custom styling.
  • Accessibility: every section is a landmark; focus moves to the first invalid field on submit.
  • Dark mode uses the existing commerce tokens.
  • Motion: 150ms section expand, respects reduced-motion.
  • Copy review with legal is scheduled for 2026-10-06.

    API->>P: price(items, country, coupon)
    P-->>API: subtotal, shipping estimate, discount
    API-->>C: quote

    API->>PSP: authorize(amount, wallet token)
    PSP-->>API: authorized
    API-->>C: 201 order

Pricing logic lives in one module and is shared by the quote and order endpoints:

src/pricing.ts:7-13
export function subtotalCents(items: LineItem[]): number {
  return items.reduce((sum, item) => sum + item.quantity * item.unitPriceCents, 0);
}

export function applyCoupon(subtotal: number, percentOff: number): number {
  return Math.round(subtotal * (1 - Math.min(Math.max(percentOff, 0), 100) / 100));
}
POST/v1/checkout/quotePrice the cart before an address is complete🔒 session
Field Type Required Notes
items LineItem[] yes sku, quantity
country string yes ISO 3166-1 alpha-2, used for the shipping estimate
coupon string no Returns couponError instead of failing the request

Responses: 200 quote · 422 invalid items

POST/v1/ordersCreate the order and authorize payment🔒 session

Requires an Idempotency-Key header (UUID). Replays with the same key return the original response.

Responses: 201 order created · 402 payment declined · 409 key reused with a different body · 422 validation errors (all fields at once)

Decisions

Decisionaccepted2026-09-08·@maya
Accordion layout on mobile, two-column on desktop

Won usability testing on completion rate. See the design notes for details.

Decisionaccepted2026-09-11·@api-team
Idempotency keys on order creation

Mobile retries currently create about 0.3% duplicate orders. Keys fix this and are required by the PSP for wallet payments anyway.

Decisionrejected2026-09-11·@maya
Build our own wallet integration

Our PSP already supports Apple Pay and Google Pay; building our own would add PCI scope for no benefit.

Risks

⚑Apple Pay domain verification delays launch
Impact high·Likelihood medium·Owner @payments·open

Verification needs the production domain and a certificate from the PSP. Start in week 1 and have Google Pay ready to launch alone if needed.

⚑Shipping estimate differs from the final price
Impact medium·Likelihood low·Owner @dev-checkout·mitigated

Show “estimated” until the postcode is complete. Differences above €1 trigger a visible price update with an explanation.

Analytics

Events are defined in the analytics tracking plan. The dashboard (“Checkout v2”) tracks abandonment per section, wallet usage, and time to pay. Weekly readouts go to #checkout-v2 every Monday.

Full event list
  • checkout_viewed — page load, with cart value and item count
  • checkout_section_opened — section name
  • checkout_section_completed — section name, time spent
  • checkout_field_error — field name, error code
  • checkout_coupon_applied / checkout_coupon_failed — code, reason
  • checkout_wallet_clicked — wallet type
  • checkout_payment_authorized / checkout_payment_declined — method, amount band
  • order_created — order id, time to pay

Implementation notes

🤖For agents: Engineering constraints
  • All prices are integer cents. Never use floats for money; reuse subtotalCents and applyCoupon from src/pricing.ts.
  • POST /v1/orders must be idempotent: store the key with the response for 24h, and return 409 if a key is reused with a different body.
  • Validation errors are returned all at once (422 with a fields map); the client must not stop at the first error.
  • Everything new goes behind the checkout_v2 feature flag; the old checkout must keep working untouched.
  • Every new endpoint needs an OpenAPI entry and a contract test.
  • If the open question about guest wallet buttons is unresolved, show wallet buttons only after the email field.
